1. Features Used By The Evaluation Function:

A. Opening Phase (MiniMaxOpeningImproved.py):
   1. Material Difference (numWhitePieces - numBlackPieces)
   2. Completed Mills Difference (whiteMills - blackMills)
   3. Potential Mills / 2-in-a-Row Threats (whiteThreats - blackThreats)

B. Midgame / Endgame Phase (MiniMaxGameImproved.py):
   1. Material Difference (numWhitePieces - numBlackPieces)
   2. Mobility Difference (numWhiteMoves - numBlackMoves)
   3. Completed Mills Difference (whiteMills - blackMills)
   4. 2-in-a-Row Threats (whiteThreats - blackThreats)
   5. Blocked Opponent Pieces (blockedBlackPieces - blockedWhitePieces)


2. Why They Were Selected:

- Material Difference:
Having more pieces than the opponent is a major advantage in Nine Men's Morris.
If the opponent is reduced to only two pieces, the game is immediately won.
- Potential Mills (Threats):
When two pieces are lined up with an open third spot, there is a chance to complete a mill on the next move.
This forces the opponent to react and can lead to capturing one of their pieces.
The original function did not account for these possible mills.
- Completed Mills:
Completed mills give us more control over the board and protect pieces since pieces in a mill usually cannot be captured.
- Mobility Difference:
Another way to win is by leaving the opponent with no legal moves.
Because of this, having more available moves while limiting the opponent's movement gives us a stronger position.
- Blocked Pieces:
Blocking the opponent's pieces reduces the number of moves they can make and limits their options throughout the game.


3. Feature Combinations:

Opening:
   Evaluation = (100 * MaterialDiff) + (25 * MillsDiff) + (10 * ThreatsDiff)

Midgame/Endgame:
   Evaluation = (1000 * MaterialDiff) 
              + (20 * MobilityDiff) 
              + (50 * MillsDiff) 
              + (30 * ThreatsDiff) 
              + (25 * BlockedDiff)

Terminal conditions (<= 2 pieces or 0 legal moves) immediately return +10000 or -10000.


4. Why This Evaluation Is Better Than The Original:

The original static estimation from the handout only looked at the number of pieces and the opponent's available moves (1000 * material - blackMoves).
During the opening phase, it mainly focused on piece count, so most moves that did not immediately capture a piece were treated about the same.
    
The improved evaluation considers more of what is happening on the board.
It rewards moves that create potential mills, blocks the opponent from setting up threats, and limits the movement of their pieces.
It also looks for opportunities to create multiple threats at once, which can lead to more captures and a stronger position later in the game.


5. Example Positions With The Improved Function:

Example 1: Opening Phase
Input Board: WxxWxxxxxxxxBxxxxxxxxxB
White has pieces at a0 (0) and b1 (3), while Black has pieces at f3 (12) and g6 (22).

Original Function (MiniMaxOpening.py):
Selected Board: WxxWWxxxxxxxBxxxxxxxxxB
Move: White places a piece at index 4 (d1).

Improved Function (MiniMaxOpeningImproved.py):
Selected Board: WxxWxxWxxxxxBxxxxxxxxxB
Move: White places a piece at index 6 (c2).

Explanation:
The original function only looks at the difference between the number of White and Black pieces.
Since placing a piece in any open spot without getting a capture gives the same score of 3 - 2 = 1,
the function has no way to tell which position is actually better.
Because of this, it ends up choosing the first available position, which is index 4.

The improved function recognizes that positions 0, 3, and 6 (a0 - b1 - c2) form a mill line.
By placing a piece at index 6, White completes the mill and creates an opportunity to capture one of Black's pieces.
Because the improved evaluation checks for mills and threats, this move receives a better score.

Why the New Move is Preferred:
The move at c2 is more useful because it completes a mill and gives White a capture.
Instead of placing a piece in a position that does not create an immediate advantage,
the improved function chooses a move that strengthens White's position.


Example 2: Midgame Phase
Input Board: WxxxxxxBxxWxxxxBxxxxBxW
White has pieces at a0 (0), c3 (10), and g6 (22), while Black has pieces at e2 (7), d4 (15), and a6 (20).

Original Function (MiniMaxGame.py):
Selected Board: xWxxxxxBxxWxxxxBxxxxBxW
Move: White slides from a0 to d0.

Improved Function (MiniMaxGameImproved.py):
Selected Board: WxxxxxxBxxxWxxxBxxxxBxW
Move: White moves a piece toward the center to limit Black's movement.

Explanation:
The original function mainly looks at the number of pieces and the total number of moves available to Black.
In this position, moving toward the outside of the board does not do much to limit Black's movement.
However, because the original evaluation does not consider blocked pieces or White's own mobility, it can still choose this move.

The improved function considers both White and Black's available moves and also rewards positions where Black's pieces are blocked.
Because of this, it prefers a move that limits Black's options while keeping White in a stronger position.

Why the New Move is Preferred:
A player can win Nine Men's Morris by leaving the opponent with no legal moves.
Because of this, limiting Black's movement is more useful than simply moving a piece toward the outside of the board.
The improved function is able to recognize this and choose a move that puts more pressure on Black.
